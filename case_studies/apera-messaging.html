Website Messaging — Storytelling

AI-powered machine vision for robotics

Positioning and site messaging for a machine-vision platform, written for engineers deciding whether the AI claims hold up.

Client Apera.ai
Format Website copy & positioning
Audience Robotics & automation engineers
The Situation

An AI claim, evaluated by people who test AI claims for a living

Apera.ai's machine-vision platform made real technical claims about what its AI could do for robotic automation — but the audience reading the website was made up of engineers who evaluate AI claims skeptically and can tell when language is doing the work specs should be doing.

Generic AI marketing language would have actively undercut credibility with exactly the buyers the site needed to convince.

The Approach

Writing for the reader who's trying to poke holes in the claim

The messaging was built to survive scrutiny from a technical reader, not just to sound impressive to a general audience.

  • Grounded every AI claim in a specific robotics use case, rather than describing the technology in the abstract.
  • Wrote to the engineer's actual evaluation criteria — reliability, integration, and failure modes — instead of leading with novelty.
  • Cut language that reads as overselling to a technical audience, even where it might have tested well with a less skeptical reader.
